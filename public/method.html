How to read this site

StreetLens is an independent Cambridgeshire & Peterborough prototype. It pulls together free public datasets so you can explore an area before you move, rent or report. It is not affiliated with any police force or public body.

What the map shows

  • Police anonymise each crime to a nearby street or landmark. A pin is not the exact address of an offence.
  • Postcode views count crimes within a straight-line radius you choose (½, 1 or 2 miles) and a time window you choose (last 3, 6 or 12 months). That circle is not a police beat.
  • Heat columns count crimes in roughly 200 m squares. Taller and redder means more recorded crimes in that square.
  • Only crimes published with a location are included. Some recorded crimes never appear on the map.

Rates per 1,000 residents

We divide recorded crimes by ONS mid-year resident population, then multiply by 1,000. Busy centres look high because visitors, shoppers and workers are not in the resident count. District and LSOA rates use fixed census areas; the radius count around a postcode does not.

Solve rate

Share of non–anti-social-behaviour crimes whose latest outcome is charged/summonsed or an out-of-court resolution. Recent crimes may still be under investigation, which lowers the apparent solve rate.

Other layers

  • Schools, GPs, dentists, bus and rail come from DfE, Ofsted, NHS ODS and NaPTAN. Distances are straight-line.
  • House prices are medians of HM Land Registry standard residential sales for the latest 12 months of data.
  • Flood, broadband and deprivation (where shown) use Environment Agency, Ofcom and MHCLG open data for the postcode or its LSOA — not a property survey.
  • Parks and playing fields are centroids from OS Open Greenspace (OGL). A pin is the site’s centre, not an entrance, and the dataset covers publicly mapped greenspace rather than every open field.
  • Stop and search figures are Cambridgeshire Constabulary records published with a location. Outcomes are as recorded; most stops result in no further action. This is not a crime rate and is not broken down by personal characteristics on this site.

Year-on-year change

Where 24 months of data are available, we compare the latest 12 months with the 12 months before, for the county, each district, and each police crime type. A percentage is only shown for a type when the previous year had at least 20 recorded crimes of that type — small counts can swing by a large percentage. A change can reflect recording practice and police activity as well as offending. The overall figure is the change in all recorded crimes, not an average of the type percentages.

Time window on a postcode

The postcode dashboard defaults to the latest 12 months of published data. You can switch to the last 3 or 6 months; counts, charts, map markers, 3D columns and outcomes then use only those months. Share links can include &w=6 (or 3). District rates and year-on-year figures always use the full 12-month (or 24-month) official window, not the shorter control.

Neighbourhood rate and comparison

The LSOA rate is an approximate figure. We assign each published crime to the nearest 2021 LSOA population-weighted centroid, then divide by the LSOA’s mid-year population. Because police anonymise locations, this is not an official police LSOA statistic. Town centres often look high: visitors, shoppers and nightlife are not in the resident count.

“Lower recorded rate than X% of local neighbourhoods” is the share of Cambridgeshire & Peterborough LSOAs with a higher approximate rate than this one. It is a rank, not a safety score, and it is not adjusted for mixed use or deprivation. Nearby suggestions list other LSOAs within about five miles whose approximate rate is lower, with same-district areas first. They are not a recommendation to move.

Month grid

The year-at-a-glance grid colours each published month relative to the highest month on that chart. Police data is monthly only: there is no day-of-week or time-of-day detail on this site.

Place search, saved areas and feeds

You can type a postcode or a place name (for example Ely or Huntingdon). Place search uses postcodes.io and then opens the nearest postcode still in Cambridgeshire or Peterborough. Full street-address search is not offered, because that needs a paid or keyed service.

Save area stores a short list on this device only (it does not create an account or send alerts). District Atom feeds update when StreetLens publishes a new monthly snapshot. They are not live incident alerts. For official messages from Cambridgeshire Constabulary, use eCops.

Embeds, downloads and the crime table

The embed snippet is a compact summary that links back to the full profile. CSV and JSON downloads repeat the published district figures. The “crimes as a table” view is the same records as the map, grouped by month, type and latest outcome, so the page stays usable without the map. Locations remain anonymised, so it is not a list of addresses.

Installing the site

StreetLens can be installed from your browser as a small app. The shell is cached so the overview can open offline; live lookups (postcodes, neighbourhood team) still need a connection, and a newly published month appears once the network fetch succeeds.

Lag and updates

Police data is usually published about two months after a month ends. StreetLens checks for a new month every Wednesday and refreshes when one appears.

Season and type mix

The “usual pattern” chart shows each month as a percentage of that area’s own monthly average (100 is a typical month). A county line, when shown, uses the same months for Cambridgeshire & Peterborough. The type-mix chart compares the share of recorded crimes by type with the county mix. It is not a rate per resident: a higher share of shoplifting does not on its own mean a higher rate.

What happens after a report

The funnel starts with all recorded crimes in the view, then the share that have an outcome category (anti-social behaviour is published without one), then the share charged, summonsed or resolved out of court — the same group used in the solve rate. The list below is the latest recorded outcome, not a courtroom result. Newer months still have many cases under investigation.

Most recorded locations

Each row is a street or landmark the police published as the anonymised snap point for one or more crimes (usually labelled “On or near …”). It is not the exact address of an offence. Two rows can share a name when the force uses the same label at different points, such as two parking areas. These 12-month totals do not follow the 3- or 6-month dashboard window.

District and neighbourhood league

A trend sparkline on each neighbourhood row shows how assigned monthly counts moved over the latest 12 months. It is a shape from the same nearest-centroid assignment as the rate, not a second official total, and the table cannot be sorted by the sparkline. The league page lets you sort districts and LSOAs by recorded rate, count, year-on-year change and, for neighbourhoods, the percentile rank. The neighbourhood rank is not a safety score. Town centres often sit high because rates use resident population. LSOA rates remain approximate (nearest-centroid assignment).

Road collisions

Injury collisions come from the Department for Transport’s STATS19 road safety data for the calendar years 2024 and 2025. Only personal-injury collisions on public roads that were reported to the police are included; damage-only incidents are not. Locations are as reported, not police crime snap points. Severity is as recorded — reporting practice can affect the serious/slight split. These years are not the same window as the monthly crime file.

Neighbourhood teams

Team names, 101 or email contacts, published priorities and named officers come from the data.police.uk neighbourhood API for the force area that contains the postcode. Upcoming events are listed from the same API’s events feed. StreetLens does not edit this content. It changes when the force updates it. For a non-emergency report, use 101 or the force website; call 999 in an emergency.

Summary card

The Card button builds a PNG on your device from the figures already on screen so you can save or share a snapshot. It is not an official certificate or a safety score.

Neighbourhood events

Upcoming beat meetings, surgeries and drop-ins are fetched live from the data.police.uk neighbourhood events API for the team that covers the postcode. Dates are shown in Europe/London time, as the force published them. A registration link is shown only when the force included a web address in the event details. The list can be empty even when meetings exist, if the force has not published them on that API.

Households and council tax

The household card is background about who lived in the neighbourhood at Census 2021 (tenure and a short age profile, from ONS tables TS054 and TS007A via Nomis) and how many domestic properties sat in each council tax band at 31 March 2026 (Valuation Office Agency, CTSOP1.1). It is not a judgement and not a safety score. Census figures are from 21 March 2021 and will have changed. Council tax bands in England rest on 1991 values. Band counts are rounded to the nearest 10 in the source, and some small counts are suppressed.

Crime near schools and stations

On a postcode page, each nearby school and rail station shows how many recorded crimes in the chosen time window have an anonymised pin within 250 metres (about 0.16 miles) of that site. Pins are snapped to a public location, so this is not a count of incidents on the school or station site.

Between two postcodes

When both sides of the compare page have a Cambridgeshire or Peterborough postcode, StreetLens counts recorded crimes whose pin falls within 250 metres of the straight line between the two postcode centroids. This is not a driving, walking or public-transport route, and it is not a commute time. The same time window as the compare page applies, and pins are anonymised in the usual way.

Explaining a figure

Short plain-English notes sit under selected totals, rates, solve figures, neighbourhood rates, outcome funnels and collision counts. They restate what the number includes and what it does not. They are not a second dataset.
